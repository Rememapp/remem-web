A serious job search means somewhere between 50 and 200 applications. Almost every one runs through an ATS — Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, Ashby — and almost every one asks for the same things: your employment history, your education, your address, your eligibility to work.

At fifteen to twenty minutes per application, retyping information you've already written is a tax of entire working days. Paid in your least motivated hours.

## Why "just upload your résumé" doesn't save you

Most ATS platforms parse your CV and then ask you to fix everything they got wrong, field by field. The parse is a starting point, not a solution — you're still proofreading a robot's homework on every single application.

## What actually helps

The fix isn't automation — you don't want a bot mass-applying in your name, and neither do employers. The fix is memory:

1. **Store your history once**, in a structured profile you control.
2. **Fill each application in one reviewed click**, whatever the platform calls its fields.
3. **Draft the open-ended answers** — the "why us?" questions — from your real experience, then edit them in your own voice.

That last step matters. A cover letter should be yours; a helper should hand you a solid first draft, not send generic text on your behalf.

## Keep your data where it belongs

Your work history, salary expectations, and visa status are sensitive. A tool that uploads them to someone's server to "help you apply faster" is trading your privacy for its convenience. Remem keeps the whole profile on your device — the only thing that ever leaves is the context you choose to send when you ask for a draft, and it isn't persisted.

Apply to fewer jobs, better — and let a private memory handle the part that was never worth your attention.
